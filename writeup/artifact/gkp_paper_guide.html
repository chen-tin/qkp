01 / The contribution

Use the error record to improve the machine.

Sivak et al. use QEC detections to recalibrate Willow’s controls. Measurements support both decoding and hardware optimization. Each epoch samples Gaussian-distributed control settings, runs QEC, and rewards lower detection rates. Local detector–parameter relationships restrict gradient updates. The controller learns distribution means and widths; its policy is not a neural decoder. [1, Fig. 2; Methods]

Experiments cover distance-5/7 surface codes and distance-5 colour codes. Under injected drift, control steering reduces mean logical error 24%; adding decoder steering gives 31%. Stability improves 2.4× and 3.5×, respectively, measured by reduced standard deviation. [1, Fig. 4]

Reported per-cycle errors: 7.72(9) × 10−4 (distance-7 surface, AlphaQubit2) and 8.19(14) × 10−3 (distance-5 colour, Tesseract). These combine multiple advances; separate fine-tuning tests show about 20% improvement. [1, Fig. 3]

Evidence boundary: hardware trials repeatedly prepare short memories. Uninterrupted steering and distance-15 scaling are simulated. The demonstrated decoder steering requires logical-error estimates. Detector rate is a proxy; logical performance needs checking. [1, Figs. 4–5]

02 / Build the intuition

Correction and calibration answer different questions.

Think of a measurement record as evidence with two possible uses. A decoder asks which error best explains the observed pattern. A calibration procedure asks whether changing a physical setting improves future observations. Earlier experiments already connected error detection to calibration, and model-free quantum control learned settings from measurement-based rewards. These are the two ideas to keep in mind while reading. [3] [5]

InferenceObservations→Error hypothesis→Correction
OptimizationTrial settings→Measured reward→Revised settings

A small teaching example: suppose setting A produces 70 detections in 1,000 detector opportunities and setting B produces 50. Their estimated detection rates are 0.07 and 0.05. A reward equal to minus the rate prefers B. Repeated trials are necessary because random sampling could make either setting look better once. These are invented numbers illustrating an objective, not measurements from the paper.

For a simple search distribution, write a proposed setting as θ = μ + σz, with z ∼ N(0, 1). Here μ is the current centre and σ sets how far proposals explore. A reward-weighted update can move the centre toward promising proposals. This is an explanation of parameter search; it is not the project’s AR(1) disturbance generator. In the playground, σq describes environmental kicks, whereas σ in this example describes deliberate exploration.

Policy-gradient methods adjust a sampling policy from rewards. PPO, a method introduced in 2017, uses a surrogate training objective to limit disruptive policy changes while reusing collected samples. “Surrogate” here refers to the optimizer’s training objective; a detector-rate proxy for logical performance is a separate modeling choice. [4]

03 / Read the numbers

An error, an error probability, and a fluctuation are different.

A detection event is an observed warning. A logical failure means the encoded information is wrong after recovery. Successful correction can therefore coexist with many detections. The practical value of a detector statistic is that it is observable frequently, while a reliable logical memory should fail rarely. Earlier surface-code experiments distinguish detection statistics from logical performance. [8]

Mean

Average error level across evaluations.

Standard deviation

How much that level varies between evaluations.

Per-cycle rate

A rate inferred for one correction cycle under the measurement model.

Illustrative arithmetic: if a mean error changes from 0.010 to 0.007, the reduction is 30%. If its standard deviation changes from 0.0035 to 0.0010, stability improves by a factor of 3.5. Those statements describe different features of the same distribution.

QKP’s logical_error_rate() reports the fraction of trials with odd residual lattice parity at each round. Its value at round T is a horizon probability, not automatically an independent failure rate per cycle. If independent flips each occurred with probability ε, parity would give PL(T) = [1 − (1 − 2ε)T]/2. This follows because two flips cancel. Correlated noise and history-dependent recovery can invalidate that simple model; dividing a plotted probability by T is not generally a justified conversion. [QKP scoring code]

04 / Connect it to the code

Where our project sits in this picture.

The connection is a useful division of responsibilities: representation defines the protected state, inference interprets evidence, and control changes the physical process. QKP lets us examine the first two in a tractable model. The table describes the checked code, rather than a claim of hardware equivalence.

Part of QKPWhat it actually doesWhat this lets us study
EncoderConstructs an approximate logical-zero GKP ket using a coherent-state sum, an energy envelope, and normalization.Finite-energy state shape; it is not an optimized preparation pulse sequence.
Noise engineGenerates correlated displacement kicks and independent measurement noise; extensions change the noise over time.Memory and mismatch under specified stochastic assumptions.
DecoderMaps syndrome history to corrections. Bayes tracks hidden-state probabilities; the LSTM predicts parity targets.How much history helps recover the logical parity.
SimulatorTracks a q displacement, wraps observations, subtracts corrections, and scores residual parity.Decoder behavior in the shift model, without evolving the encoder’s full quantum state each round.

Code: encoder · noise · LSTM · Bayes filter · simulator

Our interpretation: a possible extension would add a slower calibration loop around the existing correction loop. It would need controllable parameters that change the observation or disturbance process, drift independent of those controls, and an observable reward. Simply letting an agent lower sigma_q would give it direct access to the noise generator rather than model a physical actuator. This is a proposed direction, not an implemented result.

Comparing performance would also require matching the protected information, error channels, readout, horizon, and scoring convention. The viewer’s shift-model percentages alone cannot establish a hardware advantage.

05 / Prior work

Seven useful steps through the literature.

This is a selected reading trail. Some entries are methodological predecessors; others explain the GKP and decoding concepts needed to connect the paper to QKP.

  1. 2001

    Gottesman, Kitaev & Preskill: encode a qubit in an oscillator.

    The GKP construction places a discrete logical code inside a continuous-variable system and protects against small q and p shifts. This supplies the encoding background for our lattice plots. QKP’s one-quadrature shift model studies a restricted part of that protection problem. [2]

  2. 2016

    Kelly et al.: calibrate using error detection.

    On nine qubits, this work optimized gates during repetitive error detection and compensated injected frequency drift. It establishes an earlier experimental link between calibration and QEC measurements; feedback from error signals has a history preceding the 2026 paper. [3]

  3. 2017

    Schulman et al.: make policy-gradient updates practical.

    PPO alternates gathering experience and optimizing its policy objective. It is general reinforcement-learning methodology, not a quantum code or decoder. Read it to understand why controlling the size of a learning update matters. [4]

  4. 2022

    Sivak et al.: learn quantum controls from measurements.

    This work develops model-free learning of parameterized control circuits for an oscillator coupled to an ancilla. Its demonstrations in simulation include state preparation and logical gates. It explains the measurement-and-reward approach without requiring an exact dynamical model for the optimizer. [5]

  5. 2023

    Sivak et al.: GKP correction beyond break-even.

    A superconducting cavity stores a GKP qubit, with an ancilla and reinforcement-learning-optimized correction. The reported coherence gain is 2.27 ± 0.07 relative to the best passive encoding in the system. This is the closest experimental bridge to our encoder; it includes quantum dynamics and control overhead absent from our shift simulator. [6]

  6. 2024

    Bausch et al.: learn to decode the syndrome history.

    AlphaQubit uses a recurrent transformer, pretrained on simulated data and fine-tuned on experimental records, to decode surface-code errors. Its connection to our LSTM is the inference task: use measurement history to predict logical errors. The codes, architectures, and physical noise models differ. [7]

  7. 2025

    Google Quantum AI: demonstrate below-threshold memories.

    Willow experiments show logical errors decreasing with increasing surface-code distance, and include a distance-5 memory with real-time decoding. This supplies hardware context for maintaining performance as a code grows. Published online in December 2024; the journal issue is 2025. [8]
