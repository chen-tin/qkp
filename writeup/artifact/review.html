GKP decoding under correlated displacement noise: a validated simulator and its benchmarks

The supporting material for a paper. It covers the noise model and shift-model simulator, the evidence that they are correct, how they compare with real hardware, and what the decoders achieve. 1Model: one quadrature, classical shifts, correlated and drifting noise

Square GKP code, lattice spacing L = √π, q quadrature only. The displacement εt is an AR(1) process whose parameters may change per round. Its innovation is scaled so the variance stays σq(t)² and the lag-1 correlation is ρ(t). The readout error νt is independent each round.

εt = ρt(σt/σt−1) εt−1 + σt√(1−ρt²) zt,   νt ~ N(0, σm,t²)

The nominal point is σq = 0.20 L, σm = 0.05 L, ρ = 0.9. Section 3 shows that this is a stress regime: about 7× the noise variance of a state-of-the-art cavity.

Scope. Not modelled: finite energy (but see §2), p-quadrature noise, ancilla errors, continuous corrections. Real cavity hardware also reads one ancilla bit per round, not an analog syndrome; this matters when comparing with experiments.

Ground truth comes in three tiers

  • exact Closed forms for nearest lattice: single round, the per-round marginal for any ρ, PL(n) at ρ = 0, and the ρ → 1 limit.
  • optimal The Bayes grid filter given the true model is the optimal syndrome-only decoder for parity corrections. The 64- and 128-point grids agree on 99.9995% of trial-rounds. Under drift, the oracle (true schedule each round) takes this role.
  • missing No closed form for PL(t) at 0 < ρ < 1, and no public GKP syndrome data.

Numerics and statistics are not limiting

  • Precision: float32 with TF32 changes the Bayes posteriors by at most 4×10⁻⁴. Decisions change only at near-ties, and the effect is at least 100× below the statistical error.
  • Error bars: across 30 independent seeds, the seed-to-seed scatter ÷ the reported SE is 0.73–1.12, with every 95% interval containing 1 except the Bayes flip rate (0.58–0.98, conservative).

50-round runs at the nominal point, where PL stays small and the flip rate is a valid per-round error. It is not valid for long runs: once PL nears ½, revising decoders dither and every dither counts as a flip. Long runs therefore use the error per round fitted from the decay of 1 − 2PL(t). At about 10⁻⁵ per round over 1 000 rounds, that needs 2 000–9 500 trials for 10% precision (benchmark C1c).

5Robustness and coverage: drifting noise does not break the Bayes filter, but hardware sits outside every scenario we simulate

Stress test: the fixed nominal filter under all 16 nonstationary schedules

The filter assumes stationary nominal noise (σq = 0.20 L, ρ = 0.9) while the true noise drifts, steps, switches or bursts. 2 000 trials × 1 000 rounds per point (IMM: 1 000). There were no belief collapses at any point, and the filter beats nearest lattice everywhere. Its error per round is 0.97–1.48× the oracle's. The largest losses come where the noise leaves the assumed range or correlation vanishes. The adaptive IMM removes most of the loss, except under 1/f drift (1.43×). After a step to ρ = 0, which is outside its grid, its fitted error per round matches the oracle but its PL trails by 0.046 (2.2σ).

An earlier version of this test scored decoders by logical flip rate, which made every Bayes filter look worse than nearest lattice when ρ dropped. That came from PL saturating, not from the decoders; see the note in §2.

Coverage: hardware-scale noise is a regime the decoder comparisons have not tested

Figure 7. (a, b) Parameter space: our sweep points, the 5–95% ranges of the E9–E11 schedules, and the LSTM training box. Hardware (Sivak et al.) has σq = 0.072 L, below everything we simulate, and its ρ has never been measured. (c) What decoders see. At hardware scale, readout noise dominates, so consecutive syndromes are anti-correlated (lag-1 correlation −0.24 to −0.38 for any ρ). All our scenarios sit at +0.23 to +0.58, except ρ = 0 (−0.04), 2.1–2.8 standardised units away. There, any memory advantage would come from averaging readout noise rather than tracking correlated drift. Details: docs/benchmarks.md §H.
